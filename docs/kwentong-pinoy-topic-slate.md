# THE 30 SHORTS (5 scenes each, ~55–75s)

## Cluster A — Mga Nilalang ng Dilim (creatures beyond the usual aswang roster)

**1. ★ Berbalang — Ang Aswang na Iniiwan ang Sariling Katawan**
Hook: SA CAGAYAN, MAY ASWANG NA NATUTULOG — PERO ANG KALULUWA NIYA, NANGANGASO.
Angle: The Berbalang of Cagayan de Oro leaves its body at night, flying as a disembodied spirit with glowing eyes; 19th-century accounts by British officer Ethelbert Forbes-Skelton documented villagers who swore by it. The defence: coconut vinegar and a blade by the sleeping body — kill the body and the spirit can never return. Depth: a creature with named witnesses and a written record, not campfire filler.

**2. Busaw — Ang Kumakain ng mga Patay (Manobo, Davao)**
Hook: HINDI BUHAY ANG HANAP NIYA — KUNDI ANG BAGONG LIBING.
Angle: The Busaw looks human by day, lives near graveyards, and digs up the freshly buried; Manobo tradition says it can smell a dying person from villages away. Families held longer wakes and guarded graves for nights. Depth: a whole funeral culture — extended lamay, grave guards — built around one creature.

**3. Ekek — Ang Ibong may Mukha ng Tao**
Hook: KAPAG NARINIG MO ANG "EK... EK... EK..." SA DILIM, HUWAG KANG LALABAS.
Angle: The Visayan Ekek is a huge bird with a human face and arms under its wings; its cry mimics a lost traveller calling for help — step out to help, and you're the prey. Depth: the monster weaponises kindness; the story turns on a listener who almost opened the door.

**4. Sarangay — Ang Toro ng Ilocos at ang Hikaw na Mahiwaga**
Hook: MAY GINTONG HIKAW ANG TORONG ITO — AT SINUMANG MAGNAKAW NITO, MAMATAY.
Angle: The Ilocos Sarangay is a bull-bodied guardian of hidden treasure wearing a jewelled earring; farmers who found it in their fields faced a choice — greed or survival. Depth: a Philippine minotaur guarding wealth, with a moral engine: everyone who tried to take the earring died rich for exactly one night.

**5. Boroka — Ang Mangkukulam na may Pakpak ng Paniki (Ilocos)**
Hook: SA ARAW, SIYA AY ISANG LOLA. SA GABI, SIYA AY BAGYONG MAY PAKPAK.
Angle: The Boroka flies as a hag with bat wings and eats children and the sick; Ilocos elders named specific women suspected of being Boroka — and the accusations ruined real lives. Depth: where the creature story bleeds into real witch-hunt history.

**6. Bungisngis — Ang Higanteng Hindi Mapigil ang Tawa (Batangas)**
Hook: NARIRINIG MO MUNA ANG TAWA NIYA BAGO MO SIYA MAKITA — AT HULI NA ANG LAHAT.
Angle: A one-eyed giant whose endless laughter shakes trees — but in the tales, clever villagers outwit him because he literally cannot stop laughing at the wrong moment. Depth: a monster you beat with humour; a rare Pinoy creature story where wit, not garlic, is the weapon.

**7. Kubot — Ang Aswang na Nakatago sa Buhok**
Hook: AKALA MO PANIKI. HINDI — ASWANG NA BALOT NG MAHABANG BUHOK.
Angle: The Kubot hangs upside down from trees and roofs, a tangle of hair with wings, waiting to drop on whoever walks beneath. The only warning is the soft flap nobody looks up at. Depth: pure dread mechanics — the monster lives where nobody ever looks: above.

**8. ★ Magindara — Ang mga Sirena ng Visayas**
Hook: MAY KUMAKANTA SA ILALIM NG DAGAT — AT ANG MANGINGISDA, HINDI NA UMUUWI.
Angle: The Magindara are beautiful water spirits whose songs pull fishermen to drowning reefs; old sailors made offerings before casting nets, and blamed specific reefs on specific songs. Depth: a mermaid tradition darker than any fairy tale — the sea itself as a hunter, with named reefs and real drowned men behind the lore.

## Cluster B — Mga Lugar na Hindi Tahimik (haunted places with documented bones)

**9. ★ Corregidor — Ang Isla na Hindi Sumuko, Kahit Patay Na**
Hook: SA ISLANG ITO, ANG MGA SUNDALO AY NAGBABANTAY PA RIN — 80 TAON PAGKATAPOS NG DIGMAAN.
Angle: The fortress island where thousands of Filipino and American soldiers died in 1942 and 1945; tunnel hospitals, the Malinta Tunnel, guards and tour guides reporting footsteps, voices, and figures in uniform to this day. Depth: the haunting is inseparable from real sacrifice — history first, ghosts second. (Wave 2 backup, now promoted.)

**10. Fort Santiago — Ang mga Bilanggo ng Baha**
Hook: NOONG 1945, DAAN-DAANG BILANGGO ANG INILOCK SA ILALIM NG FORT SANTIAGO — AT PINASOK NG TUBIG ANG KANILANG SELDA.
Angle: Japanese forces held prisoners in the fort's dungeons as Manila burned; when the fort was retaken, the cells told the story. Rizal also walked from here to Bagumbayan. Depth: one location carrying both the national hero's last steps and a mass grave — the ghosts reported here have names and dates.

**11. Laperal White House — Ang Puting Bahay ng Baguio**
Hook: ANG PINAKAMAGANDANG BAHAY SA SESSION ROAD, WALANG GUSTONG tumira.
Angle: A 1920s American-era mansion turned Japanese garrison headquarters in WWII — and Baguio's most famous haunted address, now a bamboo museum that visitors still photograph figures in. Depth: beauty, war crimes, and a haunting in one postcard-perfect house.

**12. Clark Air Base Hospital — Ang Ospital na Iniwan ng Amerika**
Hook: INIWAN NILA ANG OSPITAL NOONG 1991 — PERO MAY NAIWAN PA RIN SA LOOB.
Angle: Abandoned after Pinatubo, the vast hospital complex became a pilgrimage site for ghost hunters across Asia; guards report stretchers moving and voices in the morgue wing. Depth: a ruin of empire — the haunting story is also a story about what America left behind.

**13. Loakan Road — Ang Babaeng Sumasakay sa Baguio**
Hook: SA LOAKAN ROAD, MAY BABAENG SUMASAKAY SA TAXI — AT NAWAWALA BAGO MAKARATING.
Angle: Baguio's vanishing hitchhiker: taxi drivers on Loakan Road pick up a quiet woman in white who disappears mid-ride — a story drivers have told for decades, tied to a real girl's death on that road in local telling. Depth: a living urban legend with working witnesses — the drivers still warn each other today.

**14. UPLB — Si Mariang Banga at ang mga Multo ng Los Baños**
Hook: SA CAMPUS NA ITO, MAY ESTUDYANTENG NAGHAHATID NG BANGA — MAGPAHANGGANG NGAYON.
Angle: The University of the Philippines Los Baños carries stacked lore — Mariang Banga at the bridge, the baker who still works at the old bakery, wartime ghosts from when the campus was an internment camp for 2,000+ prisoners. Depth: student folklore layered over a real WWII camp — two histories haunting one campus.

**15. Paco Cemetery — Ang Sementeryong Bilog ni Rizal**
Hook: DITO UNANG INILIBING SI RIZAL — PALIHIM, SA HINDI MINARKAHANG LUPA.
Angle: The circular cemetery where Rizal's body was secretly buried after his execution, where three martyr priests (Gomburza) also rest, and where children were interred in wall niches during epidemics. Depth: the most beautiful cemetery in Manila is also a map of the nation's martyrs.

**16. Manila Metropolitan Theater — Ang Multo ng Art Deco**
Hook: ANG PINAKAMAGANDANG TEATRO NG 1931, NAGING BAHAY NG MGA KALULUWA NOONG DIGMAAN.
Angle: The Met survived the war as a shelter and worse; restoration workers and performers report a lady in the balcony and music from empty halls. Depth: a building that mirrors Manila itself — glory, ruin, and a stubborn refusal to stay dead.

## Cluster C — Madilim na Kwentong Bayan (folk tales and cases with real blood in them)

**17. ★ Teniente Gimo — Ang Handaan sa Aklan (from the original slate)**
Hook: MAY IMBITASYON SIYA SA PISTA — PERO HINDI SIYA ANG HANDA, IKAW.
Angle: Capiz/Aklan lore's most notorious aswang: dressed as a respectable lieutenant, he invited a young woman visiting for the fiesta to his house — where his family waited, and she was meant to be the feast. In the classic telling she escapes by swapping places with his own daughter. Depth: the horror is social — the monster is the trusted host, and the tale warns about strangers' hospitality in fiesta season.

**18. Maria Labo — Ang Aswang ng Capiz (from the original slate)**
Hook: UMALIS SIYA BILANG NARS. UMUWI SIYA BILANG ASWANG.
Angle: The Capiz legend of a woman who went to work abroad and came home changed — in the darkest version, her own children discover what she became. Depth: an OFW story at its core: what distance and desperation do to a family, told as a monster tale. Capiz's "aswang capital" reputation anchors the whole piece.

**19. ★ Siquijor — Ang Isla ng mga Mangkukulam at Manggagamot (from the original slate)**
Hook: ISANG ISLA. DALAWANG KLASE NG MAHIWAGA: ANG NANGKUKULAM, AT ANG GUMAGAMOT.
Angle: The Spanish called it Isla de Fuego for the fireflies — and for the sorcery. Siquijor's bolo-bolo healers blow healing through a bamboo straw while folk healers' rivals are blamed for barang. Depth: light and dark magic on one small island, still practised openly today — the piece interviews the tradition itself, not just the fear.

**20. Bernardo Carpio — Ang Higanteng Nakakulong sa Pagitan ng Dalawang Bundok**
Hook: TUWING LUMLINDOL, SINASABI NG MATATANDA: GUMAGALAW NA NAMAN SI BERNARDO CARPIO.
Angle: The giant of Montalban (Rodriguez, Rizal), chained between two mountains by enchantment, whose struggles to break free are felt as earthquakes — a legend so durable that real tremors in Luzon still get his name. Depth: folklore as seismology — a people's explanation for the ground moving, still alive in the age of PHIVOLCS alerts.

**21. ★ Ang Aswang na Ginamit ng CIA**
Hook: NOONG 1950s, GINAMIT NG AMERIKA ANG ASWANG — BILANG SANDATA LABAN SA MGA HUK.
Angle: Documented history: CIA operative Edward Lansdale's psywar campaign drained a dead Huk fighter's blood and staged "aswang kills" to terrify guerrillas into abandoning villages. It worked. Depth: the Philippines' most feared monster, deployed by a superpower — folklore meeting Cold War records, with sources. A story nobody expects on a lore channel, and it's true.

**22. Papa Isio — Ang Babaylan na Naging Heneral (Negros)**
Hook: SIYA AY BABAYLAN. NANGUNA SIYA NG HUKBO. TAKOT SA KANYA ANG AMERIKA.
Angle: Dionisio Magbuelas — Papa Isio — led the babaylanes of Negros against Spain then America, blending amulets, prayer, and guerrilla war; he held the mountains for years. Depth: the babaylan tradition with an army — and a direct ancestor of our own narrator character.

**23. Maria Sinukuan — Ang Diwata ng Bundok Arayat (Pampanga)**
Hook: ANG BUNDOK ARAYAT AY MAY MAY-ARI — AT NAGAGALIT SIYA SA MGA MAGNANAKAW.
Angle: Kapampangan lore's mountain guardian: a fairy who lent golden utensils to the poor and punished the greedy, whose wrath explained storms over Pampanga. Depth: Makiling's fiercer sister — a nature deity with a justice system, in a province that still tells her as warning.

## Cluster D — Misteryo ng Kasaysayan (mysteries and artifacts with documents behind them)

**24. ★ Ang Laguna Copperplate — Ang Pinakamatandang Resibo ng Pilipinas**
Hook: NOONG TAONG 900, ISANG LALAKI SA LAGUNA ANG NAGKAROON NG UTANG — AT BINURA ITO SA ISANG PLAKANG TANSO.
Angle: The oldest written document in the Philippines: a copper plate recording a debt pardon for one Namwaran and his family, in Old Malay with Sanskrit and Old Javanese terms. Depth: proof of a literate, law-governed Philippines six centuries before Magellan — a whole civilisation in one receipt.

**25. Ang Code of Kalantiaw — Ang Pinakamalaking Kasinungalingan sa Libro ng Kasaysayan**
Hook: ITINURO ITO SA ATIN SA ESKWELA NG ILANG DEKADA. PEKE PALA ANG LAHAT.
Angle: The "ancient code" of Datu Kalantiaw with its brutal punishments was taught as fact for generations — until historian William Henry Scott exposed it as a 1913 fabrication. Depth: a hoax that fooled a nation, and the detective work of the historian who killed it. Meta-history with a villain and a hero.

**26. Ang Golden Tara ng Agusan — Ang Diyosang Ginto sa Putik**
Hook: NOONG 1917, ISANG BABAE ANG NAKAHANAP NG GINTONG DIYOSA SA PUTIK NG AGUSAN — AT NAWALA ITO SA AMERIKA.
Angle: A solid gold image of a Hindu goddess, found after a flood, passed through hands until it landed in a Chicago museum — where it remains, while the Philippines asks for it back. Depth: treasure, colonial extraction, and a live repatriation fight — the story isn't finished.

**27. Ang Balangay ng Butuan — Ang mga Barko ng Ating Ninuno**
Hook: ANG MGA BARKONG ITO AY NAGLAYAG NA — ISANG LIBONG TAON BAGO PA DUMATING ANG MGA GALYON.
Angle: Plank-built boats excavated in Butuan, dated as early as 320 AD, big enough for whole communities; replicas have since sailed to China and across Southeast Asia using no modern instruments. Depth: the seafaring civilisation behind the word "barangay" itself.

**28. Ang Anting-Anting — Ang Mahika ng mga Rebolusyonaryo**
Hook: NANINIWALA SILANG HINDI SILA TATABLAN NG BALA — AT LUMABAN SILA NA PARANG TOTOO IYON.
Angle: Talismans of lead, copper, and Latin prayer carried by Katipuneros, Macario Sakay's men, and Papa Isio's babaylanes; some died certain the charm had failed only because a ritual rule was broken. Depth: faith as battlefield technology — why men charged rifles holding a medallion.

**29. Ang San Diego — Ang Galyong Lumubog Kasama ang Kayamanan (1600)**
Hook: LUMUBOG ANG SAN DIEGO NOONG 1600. NATAGPUAN ITO NOONG 1991 — BUO ANG KAYAMANAN SA ILALIM.
Angle: The Spanish warship that sank off Batangas fighting the Dutch, found 400 years later with porcelain, gold, and cannons — now in the National Museum. Depth: a shipwreck detective story with the treasure on public display; every Filipino can go and see it.

**30. Ang Sto. Niño ng Cebu — Ang Imaheng Nawala ng 44 na Taon**
Hook: INIWAN NI MAGELLAN ANG IMAHE NOONG 1521. NOONG 1565, NATAGPUAN ITO NG ISANG SUNDALO — SA NASUSUNOG NA BAHAY, HINDI MAN LANG NADAMAY NG APOY.
Angle: The image given at the first baptism, lost when Cebu burned, found by Legazpi's soldier Juan de Camus in a pine box in a burning house — the oldest Christian relic in the Philippines, centre of Sinulog. Depth: five centuries of unbroken devotion with a documented chain of custody.

# THE 10 LONGFORMS (10+ min, narration + dramatised story scenes — the Filipino Myths format)

**L1. ★ Hinilawod — Ang Pinakamahabang Epiko ng Pilipinas (Panay)**
Premise: The Sulod people's epic of the brothers Labaw Donggon, Humadapnon, and Dumalapdap — gods courting goddesses, battles with monsters, voyages to the underworld. Recorded from chanters in the 1950s, it runs for days of chanting in full.
Why it sustains: Three brothers = three full story arcs; love quests, shape-shifting brides, a descent to rescue a dead brother. The Philippines' answer to the Odyssey, almost unknown to its own people.

**L2. Darangen — Ang Epiko ng mga Maranao (UNESCO Masterpiece)**
Premise: The Maranao epic cycle of Prince Bantugan — his death, his soul's journey, and his resurrection — sung around Lake Lanao for centuries and recognised by UNESCO.
Why it sustains: Bantugan's death-and-return arc is pure drama; the Darangen also encodes Maranao law, courtship, and war. A Mindanao spotlight the channel needs — told with respect, from the tradition's own text.

**L3. ★ Ang Ginto ni Yamashita — Ang Kayamanang Naglaho, at ang Golden Buddha**
Premise: The WWII treasure legend in full: Yamashita's alleged buried loot, the treasure hunters, the death traps — and the one documented case, Rogelio Roxas, who found a golden Buddha in 1971, had it seized by Marcos's soldiers, and won a US court judgment decades later.
Why it sustains: Legend vs. court record is the engine — every chapter separates what is documented from what is believed, ending on the question nobody can answer: how much is still buried?

**L4. Maria Makiling — Ang Buong Kwento ng Diwata ng Los Baños**
Premise: The complete Makiling cycle: the fairy who helped villagers, the lovers she lost (including the tale of the young man who became a priest), her withdrawal from the world, and the mountain that still bears her profile.
Why it sustains: A full tragic arc — generosity, betrayal, grief, disappearance — plus the living lore of UPLB and Los Baños at her feet. The channel's flagship diwata story deserves the long treatment.

**L5. Biag ni Lam-ang — Ang Bayaning Isinilang na Nagsasalita (from the original slate; teased in Filipino Myths Part 2)**
Premise: The Ilocos epic: Lam-ang, born already speaking, who avenges his father, dives for treasure, dies eaten by the shark Berkakan — and is resurrected bone by bone by his rooster, dog, and the maiden Ines Kannoyan.
Why it sustains: Birth, vengeance, courtship, death, resurrection — the most cinematic precolonial epic we have, scene after scene built for dramatisation.

**L6. Alunsina at Tungkung Langit — Ang Unang Pag-ibig ng mga Diyos (from the original slate; teased in Filipino Myths Part 2)**
Premise: The Visayan creation love story: Tungkung Langit, god of order, and Alunsina, the lazy goddess whose idleness sparks the first quarrel of the gods — ending with her disappearance and his creation of the world to win her back.
Why it sustains: The universe begins with a marriage argument. Tender, funny, and cosmic — a creation myth with genuine emotional stakes, and the promised follow-up Part 2's outro already sold.

**L7. Hermano Pule at ang Cofradía — Ang Pari na Hindi Pinayagan (Tayabas, 1841)**
Premise: Apolinario de la Cruz, a young man denied the priesthood because he was Indio, builds a religious brotherhood of thousands — until the colonial state answers with soldiers on Mount Banahaw's slopes.
Why it sustains: Faith, race, and rebellion with a devastating climax; a true story most Filipinos never learned, set in Quezon province — Christian's own region's history.

**L8. Ang mga Babaylan — Ang mga Pari ng Ating Ninuno**
Premise: The priestess-shamans of the precolonial Philippines: healers, mediums, and community leaders — mostly women — whom the friars hunted as witches, and who answered by leading revolts (Tamblot, Papa Isio) across three centuries.
Why it sustains: It is the origin story of the channel's own narrator. History, persecution, resistance, and survival — babaylan still practise today. The flagship "history" longform of the channel.

**L9. Bundok Banahaw — Ang Banal na Bundok**
Premise: Quezon's mystical mountain: pilgrims who believe it is a portal and a holy city, the cults and hermits on its slopes, the "Ciudad de Dios", sacred caves and springs mapped like stations of a cosmic cross.
Why it sustains: Geography as theology — every peak and cave has a sacred identity; plus Hermano Pule died at its foot (ties to L7). Living faith, still climbed every Holy Week.

**L10. Ibong Adarna — Ang Ibong Kumakanta ng Pitong Beses**
Premise: The korido epic of the Adarna bird of Mount Tabor, whose seven songs heal and turn men to stone, and the three princes — Pedro, Diego, Juan — sent to catch it.
Why it sustains: Betrayal between brothers, a hermit's tests, a descent into a well-kingdom, two princesses, and a bird worth kingdoms. The story every Filipino half-remembers from school — told whole, dark edges intact.
