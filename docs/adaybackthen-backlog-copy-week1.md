**Caption:**
THE MATCH FACTORY GIRLS WHOSE BONES GLOWED GREEN

At the Bryant and May match factory in East London during the 1880s, teenage girls worked fourteen-hour shifts dipping wooden splints into white phosphorus paste. The toxic fumes slowly penetrated tooth cavities, causing phossy jaw, an agonizing condition where jawbones deteriorated and literally glowed in the dark. In 1888, fourteen hundred matchgirls organized a historic strike and won landmark safety protections.

**Caption:**
ONE FRESH EGG FOR AN ENTIRE WEEK

During World War II in Great Britain, civilian survival depended on the official ration book. Every citizen was restricted to strict weekly allowances: two ounces of butter, two ounces of loose tea, and only one fresh egg per week.
